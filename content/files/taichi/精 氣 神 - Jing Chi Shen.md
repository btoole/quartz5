- **Jing (精)** — **Essence**
  - Refers to the body’s fundamental vital essence or constitutional energy.
  - In traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), jing is associated with growth, development, reproduction, and longevity.
  - It is often described as the deepest, most foundational life resource.
- **Qi (氣 / 气)** (often misspelled as **chi**) — **Vital energy**, **life force**, or **breath**
  - Qi is the dynamic energy that animates the body and circulates through channels (meridians).
  - It encompasses physical vitality, movement, warmth, and physiological function.
  - While “chi” is a common English spelling, **“qi”** is the modern standard pinyin romanization.
- **Shen (神)** — **Spirit**, **mind**, or **consciousness**
  - Shen refers to awareness, mental clarity, emotional balance, and spiritual presence.
  - In TCM, it is often associated with the heart and manifests in a person’s vitality, demeanor, and consciousness.

Together, they are known as the **“Three Treasures” (三寶 / 三宝, San Bao)** of Chinese philosophy and traditional medicine:

- **Jing (Essence)** → the body’s fundamental substance.
- **Qi (Vital Energy)** → the energy that animates and sustains life.
- **Shen (Spirit/Mind)** → consciousness, awareness, and the highest expression of life.

A common way to understand their relationship is:

**Jing nourishes Qi, Qi supports Shen, and Shen directs Qi while preserving Jing.**
